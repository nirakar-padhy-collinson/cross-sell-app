# Cross-Sell Decision Demo Script

## One-Line Positioning

A governed next-best-action workspace that helps bankers prioritize cross-sell opportunities using AI/ML propensity, product fit, consent controls, suppression logic, and activation queues.

## Summary Pointers

- Problem statement: Banks want deeper customer relationships, but most campaigns are broad, poorly timed, and disconnected from consent, suitability, and banker workflow.
- Current reality: Relationship managers often work from static CRM lists, manual spreadsheets, and generic campaign segments that do not explain why a customer should be contacted now.
- Business impact: Poor targeting creates low conversion, customer fatigue, compliance risk, and wasted banker capacity.
- Platform promise: Rank opportunities by propensity, relationship strength, product fit, value, and governance eligibility, then turn them into banker-ready actions.
- Buyer relevance: Small banks and fintechs can increase wallet share while showing responsible, explainable, and consent-aware growth decisioning.

## Personas To Mention

- Relationship manager: Wants the best customers to call today and a clear reason for each action.
- Growth leader: Wants higher conversion and better campaign discipline.
- Product manager: Wants product-level demand signals and next-best-product visibility.
- Compliance team: Wants consent, contactability, KYC, suitability, and suppression controls.
- Operations leader: Wants queues, prioritization, and branch or employee-level visibility.

## Banking Domain Signals To Highlight

- Responsible growth: Make it clear that a high propensity score is not enough; consent, contactability, KYC, suitability, cooldown, and service issues must also pass.
- Relationship context: Call out product holdings, salary relationship, balance, income, tenure, segment, and engagement as signals bankers understand.
- Contact policy: Explain cooldown, suppression, channel choice, and contactability as customer-fatigue and conduct-risk controls.
- Product fit: Mention product gap checks, already-held products, recommended channel, and next-best-action as practical RM workflow inputs.
- Sales operations: Use terms like RM priority queue, campaign target, digital nurture, branch allocation, employee view, and activation-ready portfolio.
- Governance: Highlight decision packets, banker notes, champion/challenger comparison, suppression reasons, and model health as controls against mis-selling.

## Banker Language To Use

- "This is not a blast campaign; it is governed next-best-action."
- "The system separates customers we should contact from customers we should suppress."
- "The RM gets a reason to call, the product to discuss, and the channel to use."
- "Growth and compliance see the same evidence trail."

## Opening Talk Track

"Most banks already know they should cross-sell, but they struggle to decide who to contact, what to offer, through which channel, and whether the contact is appropriate. This workspace turns customer data into a governed next-best-action recommendation."

## Step-By-Step Walkthrough

1. Open the app and start on `Suite Command`.
   - Say: "This is the command landing for growth decisioning and lead activation."
   - Point out model health, policy version, data quality, targetable rate, suppression rate, and banker task queues.
   - Value point: Leaders can immediately see whether the opportunity pool is healthy and actionable.
   - Domain cue: "This is the view a growth head or distribution leader needs before pushing leads to branches or RMs."

2. Explain the recommendation engine selector in the sidebar.
   - Say: "The bank can choose between a rule-based recommendation engine and a machine learning recommendation engine."
   - Value point: Banks can keep existing campaign policy while testing ML-based prioritization.

3. Show `Champion vs Challenger`.
   - Say: "This compares the current engine against the alternative engine to identify decision differences and model lift."
   - Value point: Growth and risk teams can align before changing campaign logic.

4. Open `What-if Simulator`.
   - Adjust inputs such as monthly balance, income, digital engagement, campaign recency, consent, or product holdings.
   - Say: "This shows how the recommendation changes when relationship, engagement, eligibility, or fatigue signals change."
   - Value point: This makes next-best-action logic easy for bankers and executives to understand.
   - Domain cue: "Use consent or campaign recency to show that the platform can suppress a commercially attractive lead when outreach would be inappropriate."

5. Move to `New Opportunity`.
   - Pick a scenario such as wealth priority, campaign fatigue, already holds card, no consent, or suitability hold.
   - Say: "These scenarios show both revenue opportunities and responsible suppression behavior."

6. Run the opportunity assessment.
   - Point out decision, priority score, propensity, recommended product, and channel.
   - Say: "The system does not only say who is likely to buy. It recommends the product, channel, and action."
   - Domain cue: "Frame priority score as a blend of propensity, relationship value, product fit, and governance eligibility."

7. Show the AI banker brief.
   - Say: "The banker brief turns model outputs into a practical customer conversation plan."
   - Value point: RMs get a clear reason to act, not just a ranked list.

8. Show governance and activation signals.
   - Point out consent, contactability, KYC, cooldown, product gap, suppression, and confidence indicators.
   - Say: "The system prevents high-scoring customers from being activated when the bank should not contact them."
   - Value point: This is the responsible AI and compliance moment.
   - Domain cue: "This is where a bank sees mis-selling, customer fatigue, and conduct-risk controls."

9. Show relationship context.
   - Point out current holdings, customer segment, relationship strength, engagement, and balance or income signals.
   - Say: "The recommendation is grounded in the customer's actual relationship, not a generic campaign segment."

10. Show supportive and adverse reasons.
    - Say: "The banker can see what supports the recommendation and what could block or weaken it."
    - Value point: This helps RMs trust the recommendation and act with confidence.

11. Show `Download Decision Packet`.
    - Say: "The decision packet captures the rationale, recommendation, channel, and governance status."
    - Value point: This creates evidence for campaign governance and review.

12. Open `Policy Override / Banker Notes`.
    - Say: "Bankers can add context, relationship intelligence, and escalation notes."
    - Value point: Human relationship knowledge is captured rather than lost in side channels.

13. Move to `Portfolio Overview`.
    - Use filters such as product, decision, priority band, employment type, branch, employee, or channel.
    - Say: "Managers can slice the opportunity portfolio and allocate work to the right teams."

14. Show portfolio command center.
    - Point out activation-ready rate, RM priority queue, suppression rate, average propensity, and top product.
    - Say: "This lets a growth leader see campaign readiness and governance friction in the same view."
    - Domain cue: "The portfolio is not only about sales volume; it shows how much of the base is actually eligible to activate."

15. Show banker task queues.
    - Open RM Priority Queue, Suppression Review, Consent Missing, or Cooldown Hold.
    - Say: "The app turns analytics into queues that bankers and operations teams can actually work."

16. Show portfolio charts and curated table.
    - Say: "The portfolio view shows targeting mix, channel allocation, product mix, conversion signals, and individual opportunities."
    - Value point: This supports campaign planning, branch coaching, and daily RM execution.

## Demo Moments That Should Land

- "This is not a generic campaign list; it is governed next-best-action."
- "High propensity alone is not enough; consent, contactability, suitability, and cooldown also matter."
- "The app explains why a customer is recommended and why another customer is suppressed."
- "Bankers receive actions, channels, and talking points, not just model scores."
- "Managers can move from portfolio strategy to individual lead execution."
- "The app uses banking controls like consent, KYC, contact policy, product holdings, and suppression reasons."

## Buyer Objections And Responses

- Objection: "We already have CRM campaigns."
  - Response: "This sits above CRM as a decisioning layer: it decides who, what, why, when, and through which channel."

- Objection: "Our compliance team worries about mis-selling."
  - Response: "The app includes consent, contactability, KYC, cooldown, product holding, suitability, and suppression checks."

- Objection: "Our RMs will say they already know their customers."
  - Response: "The app does not replace relationship knowledge; it prioritizes the book, explains the reason to act, and captures banker context in notes."

- Objection: "Relationship managers may ignore model scores."
  - Response: "The banker brief, reasons, relationship context, and notes make the recommendation practical and explainable."

- Objection: "We do not want a black-box campaign engine."
  - Response: "The app supports rule-based and ML engines, champion/challenger comparison, reason codes, and decision packets."

## Closing Talk Track

"This cross-sell workspace helps the bank grow responsibly. It prioritizes the right customers, recommends the right product and channel, suppresses inappropriate outreach, and gives bankers an explainable action plan. That is how AI becomes commercial growth infrastructure rather than another campaign dashboard."
